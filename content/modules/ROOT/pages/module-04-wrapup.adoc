== Two Paths, One Platform

You have now used both approaches to automating CVE remediation with Red Hat Lightwell. Here is how they compare:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| | Path 1: Quick Win | Path 2: Full Automation
| *What drives it* | Renovate (deterministic) | Ansible Orchestrator + AI agents
| *How it finds affected apps* | Scans `pom.xml` against Lightwell registry | Queries TPA with SBOM data
| *How it decides what to fix* | Bumps every dependency with an `.rhlw` version available | AI assesses risk per application; auto-approves low risk, flags high risk
| *Human involvement* | Review and merge every MR | Review only the high-risk MRs
| *ITSM integration* | None | ServiceNow ticket opened, updated throughout, auto-closed
| *Best for* | Getting started; small portfolios; teams without Ansible | Enterprises at scale; compliance-heavy environments; mixed risk portfolios
| *What you need* | Renovate operator + CI/CD | Ansible Orchestrator + TPA + AI model endpoint + ServiceNow + CI/CD
|===

Neither path is "better" — they solve different problems. Many organizations will start with Path 1 and graduate to Path 2 as their vulnerability management practice matures.

== What We Did Not Cover

This lab focused on a single CVE flowing through the pipeline end to end. In production, several additional concerns apply:

* *Multi-stage deployment* — this lab deployed straight to production. Real environments typically have test, staging, and production stages, each with its own approval gate and validation suite. ArgoCD supports this natively via ApplicationSets and sync policies.

* *Custom VEX generation* — Red Hat publishes VEX documents for its products. Customers can generate VEX for their own applications to inform scanners about which component matches are genuinely exploitable and which are not.

* *Policy-as-code* — auto-approval decisions can be driven by formal policy engines (e.g. Kyverno, OPA/Gatekeeper) rather than AI heuristics, providing deterministic, auditable approval logic.

* *Fleet-scale remediation* — when a CVE affects dozens of applications across multiple clusters, Red Hat Advanced Cluster Management can coordinate the rollout.

* *Continuous compliance* — the SBOMs, signatures, and attestations generated by the pipeline feed into compliance frameworks (FedRAMP, SOC 2, PCI-DSS) as machine-readable evidence.

== Key Takeaways

* *Lightwell eliminates the sourcing problem.* Remediated, signed builds of open source components arrive automatically — no manual version hunting.

* *SBOMs turn "are we affected?" from an investigation into a query.* TPA answers in seconds what used to take days of manual searching.

* *Automation removes the waiting.* Whether it is Renovate creating a mechanical version bump or an AI agent assessing risk and proposing a change, the time between "CVE published" and "patch deployed" drops from weeks to minutes.

* *The human stays in the loop where it matters.* Both paths preserve human judgment for high-risk decisions. The automation removes the searching and the waiting, not the accountability.
